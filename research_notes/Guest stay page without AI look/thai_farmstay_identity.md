# Thai farmstay visual identity: making a guest dashboard feel local and human (Pak Chong / Khao Yai)

## Which Thai fonts suit headings vs body, what pairings work, and when is a looped face more human or traditional?

### Takeaway
The best recent evidence (Typotheque, 2024 tests, published Feb 2025) says looped Thai is a little faster for paragraph reading and is seen as more readable at every age. It reads as "classical/formal". Loopless reads as "modern" and works best for short headlines and sparse layouts. A practical farmstay pairing is a looped or semi-formal face for body and guest instructions, and a loopless or handwritten Cadson Demak face used sparingly for headings and accents.

### Cited Findings
- Typotheque ran three experiments in Bangkok in early 2024: distance acuity (N=41, mean age 34.5), continuous reading with eye tracking (N=38) and an attitude survey (N=139, mean age 42.6). They compared Zed Text Looped, Zed Text Loopless, Thonburi and Greta Compressed. — [Typotheque, "The Contemporary Effects of Loops" (Héctor Mangas, 15 Feb 2025)](https://www.typotheque.com/research/effects-of-loops-in-thai)
- Overall, looped text was easier to read than loopless text and Thonburi at distance. Older readers saw looped characters better, and younger readers saw loopless characters better. — [Typotheque](https://www.typotheque.com/research/effects-of-loops-in-thai)
- In continuous reading, looped text was slightly but significantly faster than loopless. Age had no effect, and comprehension did not differ. — [Typotheque](https://www.typotheque.com/research/effects-of-loops-in-thai)
- Loops strongly raised perceived readability across all ages and layouts (book, website, poster, branding, UI). Looped text was linked with "classical" and "formal". Loopless was linked with "modern" and "unusual". Every age group preferred looped for text-heavy settings. Younger people preferred loopless only where there was little text. — [Typotheque](https://www.typotheque.com/research/effects-of-loops-in-thai)
- Typotheque's recommendation is looped for paragraphs and loopless for short headlines and minimal layouts. The choice should also weigh audience age and brand tone (looped means tradition, loopless means modernity). — [Typotheque](https://www.typotheque.com/research/effects-of-loops-in-thai)
- Sources disagree on sample size: a secondary write-up says "over 180" readers. The two figures are consistent if you count across all three experiments. — [Typotheque](https://www.typotheque.com/research/effects-of-loops-in-thai); [ATypI abstract](https://atypi.org/?p=34632)
- Cadson Demak was founded in 2002 and became a boutique foundry under that name in 2008. Its Google Fonts families include:
  - **Anuphan:** a loopless take on IBM Plex Thai by Mint Tantisuwanna, with Thai glyphs built from IBM Plex Sans Latin outlines, on Google Fonts since 2023 under the OFL.
  - **Prompt:** loopless Thai and Latin.
  - **Athiti:** an informal, loopless Thai and Latin family.
  - **Charm:** handwritten Thai and Latin.
  — [Google Fonts: Anuphan](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Anuphan/about); [Athiti](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Athiti/about); [Charm](https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Charm/about); [Fontsource: Anuphan](https://fontsource.org/fonts/anuphan/about)
- In 2019, 13 Thai families were added to Google Fonts and Google Workspace as part of the Thai "National Fonts" collection, which the government has used officially since 2007: Bai Jamjuree, Chakra Petch, Charm, Charmonman, Fah Kwang, K2D, KoHo, Kodchasan, Krub, Mali, Niramit, Sarabun and Srisakdi. Google says they were updated so Latin and Thai pair more harmoniously. — [Google Workspace Updates blog, Feb 2019](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2019/02/expanded-thai-fonts-editors.html)
- Sarabun is by Suppakit Chalermlarp. Its name means "documentary affairs", and it is used in the Government Gazette. A designer profile credits Bai Jamjuree (2018) to him with Google Fonts and Cadson Demak. — [Luc Devroye type design page](https://luc.devroye.org/fonts-79984.html)
- IBM Plex Sans Thai Looped is credited to Mike Abbink and Bold Monday (IBM Plex family, 2017). Figma describes it as having "open counters and even spacing". — [Figma fonts: IBM Plex Sans Thai Looped](https://www.figma.com/fonts/ibm-plex-sans-thai-looped/); [Fontsource](https://fontsource.org/fonts/ibm-plex-sans-thai-looped/about)
- A Cadson Demak senior type designer (Nalat Jaturapattarapong) points to "the art and precision behind placing vowels on Thai consonants" as the core craft of Thai type. — [Monotype, "Type Around the World: Bangkok"](https://cms-prod.monotype.com/company/thought-leadership/type-around-world-get-know-bangkok-thailand)
- ExpatDen notes that Thai and English sizes may have to be balanced by hand unless the font is well designed for both scripts. — [ExpatDen Thai font comparison (TH)](https://www.expatden.com/th/%e0%b9%84%e0%b8%97%e0%b8%a2-th/%e0%b9%80%e0%b8%9b%e0%b8%a3%e0%b8%b5%e0%b8%a2%e0%b8%9a%e0%b9%80%e0%b8%97%e0%b8%b5%e0%b8%a2%e0%b8%9a%e0%b8%9f%e0%b8%ad%e0%b8%99%e0%b8%95%e0%b9%8c%e0%b9%84%e0%b8%97%e0%b8%a2%e0%b8%9f%e0%b8%a3%e0%b8%b5/)

### Inferences
- **Body text on a guest dashboard** (house rules, directions, what to bring) is text-heavy, and many farmstay guests are families with older members. The evidence favours a looped body face: IBM Plex Sans Thai Looped, Sarabun, or a looped Noto Sans Thai Looped.
- **Headings:** Anuphan or IBM Plex Sans Thai (loopless) gives a modern but quiet heading. Anuphan pairs naturally with IBM Plex Sans Latin because it is built from Plex outlines.
- **Human touch:** use Charm (handwritten) or Athiti (informal) only for one or two "host note" moments, such as a signed welcome line. Using them for whole sections looks like a costume.
- **Avoid for this tone:** Chakra Petch is angular and techy, and Bai Jamjuree is squarish and corporate. Both read as tech or automotive rather than farm. This is my own judgement; no source rates them on tone.
- **Looped feels more human or traditional** when the content is a personal message, an instruction from the host, or anything long. Loopless suits labels, buttons, dates and prices.

### Gaps
- No source found that rates Noto Serif Thai, Chakra Petch or Bai Jamjuree on tone, or names a recommended Thai pairing from Cadson Demak itself.
- I could not confirm whether Cadson Demak contributed to the Thai glyphs of IBM Plex Sans Thai or Thai Looped. Sources only credit Abbink and Bold Monday.
- The Typotheque results are a single study, and the author calls them preliminary.

## Which Thai hospitality, farm, café and craft brands show local character, and what visual devices do they use?

### Takeaway
Well-regarded Khao Yai and northern Thai stays get their local character from real materials and vernacular forms: log walls, shingled teng wood, rattan, crochet, the sala pavilion, the farm's own crops. They also keep a restrained white and beige base so wood tones carry the warmth. Handwoven textiles such as pha khao ma (checked cloth) and regional weaving are the strongest ready-made Thai rural motifs. I found little documented website or graphic-identity work for these brands.

### Cited Findings
- **Roukh Kiri Khao Yai (Pak Chong), designed by Bangkok studio Onion:**
  - Hand-sketched villas reduced to simple geometric forms, "just like a child's drawing".
  - Log perimeter walls, barn doors, shingled wood ceilings, a sala lounge roof in Teng wood.
  - Rattan headboards, crocheted cushion covers, bamboo blinds, wood-slice coasters.
  - A palette "pared back to austere whites and beiges".
  - Mango, avocado and lemon trees and an organic garden that supplies the kitchen.
  — [City Nomads, "Designs On Asia: Roukh Kiri Khao Yai"](https://citynomads.com/designs-on-asia-roukh-kiri-khao-yai-a-farmhouse-style-resort-in-thailand-where-simplicity-meets-luxury/)
- **The Roukh Kiri sala:** the lounge is modelled on "an open pavilion commonly found in temples and on riverbanks in Thailand". A critic notes that nearby resorts lean on Thai vernacular, while Roukh Kiri's minimal villas feel less rooted in place. — [City Nomads](https://citynomads.com/designs-on-asia-roukh-kiri-khao-yai-a-farmhouse-style-resort-in-thailand-where-simplicity-meets-luxury/); [The Hotel Guru](https://www.thehotelguru.com/hotel/roukh-kiri-khao-yai)
- **Chic 39 farm stay:** the principle is "modern building, local decor". The owner paired a modern cube with materials representing local values and wisdom. **AHSA Farmstay (north):** uses everyday household items and adapted local crafts inside. — [Construction+ Asia: AHSA Farmstay](https://constructionplusasia.com/th/ahsa-farmstay); [Construction+ Asia BCI award](https://www.constructionplusasia.com/th/?p=19089)
- **Khaolak Laguna brand identity (Behance):** the logo and symbol are built from the wicker and weaving patterns of Phang Nga province. This is an example of a hotel identity drawn from local basketry (จักสาน). — [Behance: Khaolak Laguna](https://www.behance.net/gallery/92135495/KHAOLAK-LAGUNA)
- **Bangkok Marriott The Surawongse:** Thai textile weaving techniques are translated into metal in the interior (art4d, 2020). — [art4d](https://art4d.com/en/2020/11/bangkok-marriott-surawongse)
- **Pha khao ma:** found in every region of Thailand, with designs and colours that vary by locality. The Thai Cabinet approved its UNESCO intangible heritage nomination on 28 Feb 2023. One popular design is "pha khao ma roi si" (checked cloth of a hundred colours). — [Thailand PRD](https://thailand.prd.go.th/en/content/category/detail/id/48/iid/163229); [Thailand Foundation](https://thailandfoundation.or.th/?p=518103)
- **Angelmæd's Isan pha khao ma line:** handwoven, mud-dyed cloth from a Kalasin community enterprise, where the uneven natural colour and texture give each piece character. — [Time Out Bangkok](https://www.timeout.com/bangkok/shopping/eight-thai-fashion-brands-reimagining-traditional-fabrics)
- **Chiang Mai boutique hotels:**
  - A Dusit Thani College study sorts their decoration into Lanna Thai, chic and Victorian.
  - Another study lists using local art and craft products in service as a defining trait.
  - Chiang Mai makers who supply hotels include ceramicist Jirawong Wongtrangan and weaving collective Studio Naenna.
  — [JDTC study](https://so01.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/journaldtc/article/view/135495); [National Geographic, Chiang Mai craft](https://www.nationalgeographic.com/travel/article/chiang-mai-thailand-craft-scene)
- **Thai studios worth looking at:**
  - pommballstudio, Chiang Mai, has designed many of the city's cafés (art4d 2023).
  - Appart_'s HelloChurros! identity is built on hand-drawn iconographic illustrations.
  - BANG BANG studio combines branding with screen printing.
  — [art4d: pommballstudio](https://art4d.com/2023/07/pommballstudio-2); [The Dieline: Hello Churros](https://thedieline.com/hello-churros); [Behance: BANG BANG studio](https://www.behance.net/bangbang_studio)
- **Thai signage:** traditional Thai letters on temples and old shop signs are read as heritage. Cleaner styles on billboards read as the city's fast pace. Thanon Phra Athit and Yaowarat are known for old lettering and vernacular signs, such as 3D gold letters on red. — [Monotype: Bangkok](https://cms-prod.monotype.com/company/thought-leadership/type-around-world-get-know-bangkok-thailand)

### Inferences
- **Palette:** an off-white or rice-paper base, wood and teng brown, a muted green from the farm, and one accent taken from a real local textile, such as the indigo or red of pha khao ma. Pick a specific cloth or farm object and sample its colours rather than choosing a generic "earthy" palette.
- **Motifs:** a thin pha khao ma check or a woven (จักสาน) strip works as a divider or card edge, used once or twice and taken from a photo of the farm's own cloth or basket. Avoid stock "Thai pattern" vectors; the [Adobe Stock "Thai weaving border"](https://stock.adobe.com/pl/templates/thai-inspired-weaving-pattern-border/419709836) kind is exactly the generic look to avoid.
- **Hand-made marks:** a hand-drawn farm map, sketched building outlines (the Roukh Kiri "child's drawing" approach) and a signed host note give a human touch without fake textures.
- **Photography:** real materials up close (wood, rattan, crops at harvest, the sala), people doing farm things, and natural light. The quiet backgrounds in these resorts let the wood and greenery do the work.
- **Signage inspiration:** for small labels such as a "fruit ripe now" tag or a room nameplate, a painted-sign or stamped feel fits the countryside. Keep it to one device.

### Gaps
- I found no published design writeups of Khao Yai farmstay websites, such as their typography or UI, and no Thai hospitality website praised specifically for its web design. Evidence is mostly about architecture and interiors.
- The Design Anthology Khao Yai feature is paywalled ([design-anthology.com](https://design-anthology.com/story/khao-yai)).
- I could not reach Wallpaper Thailand or Behance pages for detailed Khao Yai brand work.

## What are the practical rules for Thai typography on the web (line-height, sizes, tone marks, Thai vs Latin digits)?

### Takeaway
Thai stacks vowels and tone marks above and below the consonant, on up to three sides of it. Containers need generous line-height and must not clip overflow. Thai has no spaces between words, which affects line breaking. Western digits are the everyday default for prices and dates. Thai digits read as official, traditional or "old sign".

### Cited Findings
- Thai glyphs "can surround the base consonant(s) on up to 3 sides". Spaces separate phrases, not words, so line-break and segmentation detection is hard. The W3C page leaves line-height, emphasis and italics as open questions. — [W3C Thai Layout Requirements](https://www.w3.org/TR/thai-lreq/)
- "Thai has native digits, and they are commonly used." W3C gives no rule on when to use them. — [W3C thai-lreq](https://www.w3.org/TR/thai-lreq/)
- **Clipping example:** a Discourse title with line-height 1.1 clipped Thai stacked marks such as ขึ้น. The fix was `overflow: visible` on the wrapper, and clipping depends on the font. — [Discourse meta bug](https://meta.discourse.org/t/in-title-bar-n-and-similar-diacriticals-are-clipped-in-linux/67303)
- **Line box:** vowels and tone marks stack up to two levels above and one below, so the line box must cover all of them rather than Latin ascent and descent. This is a vendor or marketing source. — [App Screenshot Studio](https://appscreenshotstudio.com/thai-app-store-screenshots)
- **Prices:** Arabic (Western) digits are recommended for prices, articles and anything involving math. Some street sellers price only in Thai digits, and dual-price venues may show Thai-resident prices in Thai digits. — [Transparent Language Thai blog](https://blogs.transparent.com/thai/?p=963); [Toute la Thaïlande](https://toutelathailande.fr/en/thai-figures)
- **2024 debate:** a Change.org campaign asked state agencies to drop Thai numerals in official papers. Its examples were "๕G" and Windows "๑0". Deputy PM Wissanu rejected it, saying Thai numerals preserve Thai uniqueness. — [Thai PBS World](https://www.thaipbsworld.com/world/540512)
- **Older readers:** at small sizes and distances, older readers do better with looped forms, so size and the looped/loopless choice interact. — [Typotheque](https://www.typotheque.com/research/effects-of-loops-in-thai)

### Inferences
- **Line-height:** use about 1.6–1.8 for Thai body text and at least 1.3–1.4 for Thai headings. Never use 1.0–1.2 on Thai, and never put `overflow:hidden` or fixed heights on text boxes such as pill buttons or truncated cards. Test with stacked words like ที่นี่, ผู้ใหญ่, น้ำ and ขึ้น. These values are common practice, not sourced.
- **Size:** Thai looks smaller than Latin at the same px. Set Thai body at 16–18px, and possibly a size-adjust when Latin and Thai fallbacks mix.
- **Line breaks:** the browser breaks Thai with dictionary segmentation (ICU). Avoid `word-break: break-all`. For headings, use `<wbr>` or non-breaking spans to keep phrases such as dates and the farm name together.
- **Emphasis:** avoid synthetic italics on Thai, since W3C flags it as an open issue. Use weight or colour instead.
- **Digits:** use Western digits for prices (฿1,500), dates and times. Thai digits could appear once as a deliberate traditional touch, such as a stamped house number, but never in data the guest must act on.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative numeric line-height or size standard for Thai on the web, from W3C, Google or Cadson Demak. The W3C document leaves these as open questions.

## What tone of voice makes Thai hospitality copy sound like a real host rather than marketing?

### Takeaway
Sources are thin, but they agree: write in a relaxed, conversational register. Use the host's own gendered particle (ค่ะ or ครับ) naturally but not on every sentence, soften with นะ, and open with a concrete image instead of adjectives. Overly formal, particle-heavy Thai reads as stiff or machine-made.

### Cited Findings
- A Thai writing guide warns that AI-drafted Thai often sounds too formal and stiff. It recommends a casual register and says to use ครับ/ค่ะ appropriately but not excessively ("overusing = stiff, robotic"). This is a single, non-authoritative source. — [skills.sh Thai writing guide](https://www.skills.sh/clawic/skills/thai)
- นะ is a common softening particle that makes statements friendlier or less abrupt. — [Ling app](https://ling-app.com/?p=513089)
- The particle depends on the speaker's gender, not the listener's. A female host writes ค่ะ and a male host writes ครับ. — [HoneyKids Asia](https://honeykidsasia.com/thailand/ka-vs-krab-thailand/)
- A Thai rental-listing guide recommends opening with a sentence that immediately creates an image, and writing about the experience. — [SmartOrder TH blog](https://www.smartorder.ai/resources/th/blog/perfect-your-rental-descriptions-for-more-bookings-th/)

### Inferences
- **Write as one named host, in the first person** ("ป้า…" or "พี่…"), using that person's particle. Mixing ค่ะ and ครับ, or using the corporate "ทางเรา" and "ท่าน", breaks the illusion.
- **Use concrete, local, seasonal details** instead of adjectives. For example, "ทุเรียนหลังบ้านเริ่มสุกแล้วนะคะ" (the durian behind the house is starting to ripen) instead of "สัมผัสประสบการณ์ธรรมชาติสุดพิเศษ" (experience special nature).
- **Give practical directions the way a person would,** with landmarks: the 7-Eleven, the temple, the big tamarind tree. Avoid lists of facility features.
- **English copy should be plain and warm,** with short sentences and no "nestled", "oasis" or "unforgettable".

### Gaps
- I found no study or guide specifically on Thai homestay or farmstay host copy, and no analysis of how Thai hosts address foreign guests. Recommendations above are inferences from general politeness-register sources.
