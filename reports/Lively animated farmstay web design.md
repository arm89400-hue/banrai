# Let the farm move, not the page

Baanrai 344 should feel lively because the site reacts quickly to each tap and shows real things changing on the farm, not because sections slide and fade as you scroll. Each kind of page needs its own approach. The marketing pages (home, activities, About) can each have one or two motions with a meaning: what is ripe this month, a photo that changes with the season or time of day, an orchard you can tap. The booking flow, the PromptPay pop-up and the admin get only fast feedback: a pressed state within 100 ms, a total that updates in place, a confirmation that stays on screen. The research supports this from both sides. Critics now name fade-in on every section, scroll-jacking, cursor tricks and parallax as signs of AI or template sites. Accessibility guidance says parallax causes nausea, and WCAG requires a pause control for anything that moves for more than 5 seconds. Booking case studies credit growth to faster, simpler engines, not to animation. The site already has a sound base (motion tokens, one ease-out curve, a global reduced-motion rule), so it needs no new library. It needs a better token structure, CSS-first motion and less of the fade-up animation it already uses. One caveat: no study measures how micro-interactions affect booking conversion, so everything here rests on usability research and practitioner consensus. It is not A/B data.

## "Scrolling already is the motion": what reads as AI, and what reads as alive

The patterns to remove are now named in public. In a March 2026 Y Combinator podcast that critiqued AI-generated startup sites, Rafael Shad asked why sections need to fade in at all, since **"scrolling already is the motion"**. The host said scroll-jacking made sites feel like "molasses" and said of a cursor-chasing button: "I'm so distracted by the button that's chasing me around the screen" ([StationLM summary of YC podcast](https://stationlm.com/analysis/a7f747d3-9216-4c92-8bf9-aaa3c5c1feae)). One critique counted **47 separate fade-ins on a single landing page** ([Feedbagel](https://feedbagel.com/post/critique-of-scroll-fade-animations-in-web-design)). Accessibility guidance agrees. Princeton warns that parallax causes dizziness or nausea even in people without vestibular disorders, and that scroll-jacking "disorients users" ([Princeton Digital Accessibility](https://digital.accessibility.princeton.edu/how/content/animations)). Lovable's 2026 guide limits parallax to campaign microsites, not mobile-first core sites ([Lovable](https://lovable.dev/guides/scrolling-designs-patterns-when-to-use)). Agent "skills" that promise "Awwwards-quality sites" now bundle GSAP, smooth scrolling and shaders ([skills.rest](https://skills.rest/skill/build-awwwards-quality-sites)). That is why split-text headline reveals and Lenis-style smooth scrolling now look generated: they are what a tool produces by default. The criticism is not unanimous. One Page Love still treats marquees as a recurring trend ([One Page Love](https://onepagelove.com/marquees)). But the owner has already been caught out by a teacher spotting AI, and on that brief the critics' list is the one to follow.

This matters for Baanrai today. The codebase applies the same `fade-up` keyframe in nine places, including whole page sections in `App.css`, `About.css`, `Booking.css` and `BookFlow.css`, at 0.7 s for hero blocks. That is the "fade in everything" pattern, and the slowest part of it lands on the booking steps, where it delays reading.

Real hospitality winners animate something different. Tengile Malamala, Awwwards Site of the Day on 3 October 2026, is photo-led "quiet luxury" with an **animated timeline on its About page**, a gallery and micro-interactions. It scored 7.60 for performance against 7.40 for animation ([Awwwards: Tengile](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/tengile-malamala-collection)). The closest peers to a small family farmstay are Vakantiehuis Coquelicots, a family holiday house whose highlighted feature is an **interactive booking calendar** that drives direct bookings ([Awwwards: Coquelicots](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/vakantiehuis-coquelicots)), and Masia Mont Rural, a rural estate built in a no-code tool with "editorial UX/UI design built to drive direct bookings" ([Awwwards: Mont Rural](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/masia-mont-rural)). Neither needed WebGL. The best orchard model is the Champagne grower Chartogne-Taillet, where **the motion is the land**: hand-drawn plot maps, with each plot's history appearing when you hover over it ([Immersive Garden](https://immersive-g.com/projects/chartogne-taillet-1)). Small farms that feel "alive" do it with data, not effects. Hollin Farms keeps a "Current Availability" page with an **"Updated …" date** ([Hollin Farms](https://hollinfarms.com/current-available/)), and Trevaskis Farm labels crops "Peak Season", "Last Chance" and "Season Slowing" ([Trevaskis Farm](https://trevaskisfarm.co.uk/blog/view/july-is-here-and-the-summer-holidays-are-in-sight)). The rule that follows is simple: **motion must carry information**, such as a state change, a direction, or something that has changed on the farm. If it carries none, leave it out.

Baanrai already has two things that change with the real world: the fruit calendar, and the guest's own dates on the stay page. Those should get the motion. The stay-page research reached the same conclusion: "spend boldness in one place", and on that page the bold element is what will be ripe during the guest's dates. A time-of-day hero is the next most honest option. The documented case is a header that rotates real building photos through the day, timed to local sunrise and sunset ([TJSL](https://tjsl.edu/news-media/2011/4220)). For Baanrai that means a morning, afternoon or dusk photo of the farm chosen by Bangkok time, with no generated sky. Live weather effects such as animated rain had no hospitality precedent in the research and would most likely look like a gimmick.

## Phones, older eyes and the 100 ms rule set the motion budget

The audience is Thai families on phones, including grandparents, and that sets hard limits. NN/g's thresholds still apply: **0.1 s feels instant, 1 s keeps the flow of thought, and 10 s is the limit of attention** ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/)). Google's INP metric rates "good" as **200 ms or less at the 75th percentile**, measured from input to the next frame painted ([web.dev INP](https://web.dev/articles/inp)). On a cheap Android phone, the "lively" feeling comes from painting a pressed state in the first frame, before any network request. A spinner or animation that arrives later does not produce it. Thai internet use among people aged 60+ rose from **18.2% in 2017 to 69.3% in 2024**, and 95% of people over 50 own a smartphone ([Thairath / CMMU](https://en.thairath.co.th/lifestyle/lifestyle45plus/2903927)). These are the guests who will tap the gold button twice if nothing happens. A CHI 2006 study found that adults aged 65-84 tapped standard-sized controls inaccurately, and that targets 50% larger helped ([Hourcade & Berkel](https://course.ccs.neu.edu/csg170/ssl/hourcade06.pdf)). In another study, at 22 pt type the older group performed as well as the young group ([PMC7330495](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7330495)). The practical floor is **48 px targets with 8 px gaps**. That is above WCAG 2.2's 24 px minimum and the 44 px AAA and Apple level ([Front-End Checklist](https://frontendchecklist.io/rules/accessibility/touch-targets)). The site's current 44 px fruit-name taps are close to this.

Accessibility law sets the second limit. **WCAG 2.2.2 (Level A) requires a pause, stop or hide control for anything that moves, starts automatically, lasts more than 5 seconds and sits alongside other content.** That covers autoplay slideshows, looping hero videos and marquees ([W3C 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)). WCAG 2.3.3 (AAA) asks that motion triggered by interaction can be turned off. It counts movement and size change but not opacity or colour, so a crossfade is the right fallback under reduced motion ([W3C 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)). The stay page's photo slideshow already checks `prefers-reduced-motion` before it plays. If it advances on its own, it also needs a visible pause control, or it should not autoplay at all. Gesture-only controls are the third limit. Every swipe needs a visible arrow or button beside it, which also fits the owner's rejection of app patterns.

The current global reduced-motion rule in `index.css` sets every animation and transition to 0.01 ms. That is safe, but too blunt. It also removes the colour change on a pressed button and the crossfade that W3C explicitly allows. A better default is to write movement inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`, so a calm page is what everyone gets unless motion is allowed. Under `reduce`, the site keeps short opacity and colour changes and keeps functional indicators such as spinners and payment-check stages working. Design-system guidance recommends this approach: slow functional feedback down rather than remove it ([U-M Library motion](https://design-system.lib.umich.edu/visual-elements/motion)).

## The booking flow earns liveliness through feedback, not entrances

Booking research points one way: feedback next to the user's finger, and nothing that delays the task. Baymard's accommodation study (992 hours of testing, 364 guidelines) found that guests decide on photos, room detail and a clear total. They **distrust any fee that appears late more than general shoppers do**, and they re-check details carefully before paying. A booking search placed below the fold made one user scroll twice to find it ([Baymard travel accommodations](https://baymard.com/research-articles/new-research-travel-accommodations)). Date pickers worked when they marked unavailable dates and showed price differences ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/research-articles/new-research-travel-accommodations)). Forcing an account caused **18-19% of surveyed shoppers to abandon** ([Baymard checkout](https://baymard.com/blog/current-state-of-checkout-ux)). On forms, Baymard's rules are: validate when the user leaves a field or once a phone number reaches its length, remove the error the moment it is fixed, and show positive checkmarks. The research also found that **31% of sites still lack inline validation** ([Baymard inline validation](https://baymard.com/blog/inline-form-validation)). NN/g adds three points: errors must never appear as toasts, messages users must see must not disappear on their own, and a success banner placed far from the button caused duplicate purchases ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/indicators-validations-notifications/)). For loading, NN/g says to show nothing under 1 s, use a skeleton for a full page and a spinner for one module, and avoid animated shimmer, which can distract ([NN/g skeletons](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/skeleton-screens/)).

These findings turn into a short set of motions. Each one confirms something the guest just did:

| Moment | Motion | Timing | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Any tap on a button or card | Darker shade plus `scale: 0.97` on `:active`, painted at once | 70-100 ms | Waiting for the network before any change |
| Choosing dates | Range fills in. Nights and total update in place with a short number tween | ~200 ms | Opening a modal, or moving the summary |
| Choosing a room | Selected card gets a green border and a check. The summary price changes where it is | 150 ms | Lifting cards with big shadows, confetti |
| Step 1 → 2 → 3 | Short crossfade or 8-12 px slide, plus a plain "ขั้นตอน 2 จาก 3" (step 2 of 3) | 200-250 ms | 0.7 s fade-up on every block |
| Field check | Green check after the guest leaves the field. Error text stays under the field until fixed | 150 ms | Errors while the guest is still typing, error toasts |
| Submit / pay | Button disables and its label changes to "กำลังส่ง…" (sending…) at once. A spinner appears only if the wait passes ~400 ms | 0 ms, then 400 ms delay | Optimistic "booked!" before the server confirms |
| Slip check | Text stages: uploading, checking with the bank, confirmed. Include an expected wait and a "pay at the farm / message us on LINE" fallback | Per stage | A bare spinner for over 10 s |
| Deposit received | One check mark drawn once, "รับมัดจำแล้ว" (deposit received), the booking code, then one next action | ≤ 400 ms, once | Looping confetti, auto-dismissing toasts |
| Chat message on the stay page | Bubble appears at once as "sending…", then "sent ✓". Retry if it fails | Instant | Typing animations, bouncing dots |

Optimistic UI belongs only in the chat. A guide to React's `useOptimistic` limits it to actions that succeed 95% of the time or more ([freeCodeCamp](https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/how-to-use-the-optimistic-ui-pattern-with-the-useoptimistic-hook-in-react/)), and a payment does not qualify. Vibration feedback is out too. WebKit removed the Vibration API in 2017, and the W3C's June 2024 report shows iOS Safari failing every test ([W3C device-apis log](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-device-apis-log/2024Jun/0061.html)).

The PromptPay pop-up deserves special care, because this is where trust is either earned or lost. PromptPay has about **80 million registrations** and handled **over 2.5 billion transactions worth nearly ฿5 trillion in March 2026** ([Stripe](https://stripe.com/resources/more/payments-in-thailand); [Bangkok Bank research](https://www.bangkokbank.com/th-TH/-/media/7987988529484de29125743bdd2ff585.ashx)). Older guests already scan QR codes and send slips every day, so the pop-up should look like what their bank app expects. It needs the fixed amount in large type, the payee name, a "save QR image" button for guests paying from the same phone, and the deposit and balance written as one sentence: "จ่ายตอนนี้ ฿X · เหลือ ฿Y จ่ายที่บ้านไร่" (pay ฿X now · ฿Y left to pay at the farm). The only motion the pop-up needs is a quick entrance from below (about 240 ms) and a calm, persistent confirmed state. Two gaps remain. The research found no data on how older users manage the save-QR-then-open-bank-app step on the same phone, and the evidence on a sticky bottom summary bar is mixed: one A/B test found that a sticky button raised add-to-cart but lowered final revenue ([cleancommit](https://cleancommit.io/ab-tests/mobile-bottom-sticky-add-to-cart-button/)). Test both with real guests rather than assuming.

## One token file, named after the farm, with motion as tokens too

Today the theme exists in name only. `index.css` defines `--brand`, `--brand-dark` and a few motion tokens, but **the rice-straw gold is hard-coded as `hsl(38, …)` 43 times across components**, and the CSS contains about 160 raw `hsl()` values. That is how a "single gold button" rule drifts. The fix uses the three-tier model that large systems share. Polaris keeps private palette tokens that are used only to build public alias tokens ([Polaris v11](https://polaris.shopify.com/whats-new/version-11-tokens)). Evil Martians recommend that components reference only semantic custom properties, that variants be derived with relative colour or `color-mix()`, and that values be authored in **OKLCH, whose lightness is perceptual, unlike HSL's** ([Evil Martians](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/oklch-in-css-why-quit-rgb-hsl)). A W3C format for design tokens reached its first stable version (2025.10) in October 2025 ([W3C DTCG](https://www.w3.org/community/design-tokens/2025/10/28/design-tokens-specification-reaches-first-stable-version/)). For a single Vite site with no Figma round-trip, though, a commented `tokens.css` does the same job.

The current colours convert cleanly (OKLCH values and contrast ratios computed for this report from the existing HSL values). Contrast decides the gold rule. **Dark green on gold is 5.13:1 and passes AA. White on gold is 2.09:1 and fails. Gold on the off-white page is 1.97:1.** Gold must therefore always be a filled button with dark-green text, never text, an icon or a thin line. Farm green on off-white is 6.21:1 and dark green 10.11:1, both safe for text. Hospitality identities that feel specific take their colours from a named material of the place, such as the dusk rock at Amara or the textiles and laterite soil at Jay Bhoomi ([Fine: Amara](https://www.wearefine.com/work/amara-resort-and-spa/); [Jay Bhoomi](https://sghosh.com/project/brand-identity-for-jay-bhoomi-retreat-luxury-hotel)). So the primitives should be named after the farm, and their values should be checked against the farm's own photos.

```css
/* tokens.css — tier 1: primitives (never used directly in components) */
:root {
  --canopy-35: oklch(0.357 0.052 160);   /* dark green, hsl(150,35%,20%) */
  --canopy-47: oklch(0.470 0.073 160);   /* farm green, hsl(150,35%,30%) */
  --straw-77:  oklch(0.770 0.082 81);    /* rice-straw gold, hsl(38,48%,64%) */
  --paper-98:  oklch(0.980 0.004 85);    /* page, hsl(40,30%,97%) */
  --ink-20:    oklch(0.22 0.02 140);     /* body text */

  /* tier 2: semantic roles (the only names components use) */
  --color-bg-page:      var(--paper-98);
  --color-text:         var(--ink-20);
  --color-action:       var(--canopy-47);  /* links, secondary buttons, selected, focus */
  --color-action-hover: color-mix(in oklch, var(--canopy-47), black 15%);
  --color-selected-bg:  color-mix(in oklch, var(--canopy-47) 12%, var(--paper-98));
  --color-cta-bg:       var(--straw-77);   /* ONE per screen */
  --color-cta-text:     var(--canopy-35);  /* white on gold fails at 2.09:1 */
  --color-season:       /* set per month from the fruit calendar; decoration only */;

  /* motion: durations by intent */
  --dur-instant: 80ms;    /* press, toggle */
  --dur-quick:   150ms;   /* hover, select, check mark */
  --dur-base:    240ms;   /* pop-up, menu, accordion, step change */
  --dur-slow:    400ms;   /* hero photo crossfade, confirmation */
  --dur-ambient: 700ms;   /* marketing pages only, never in booking */

  /* motion: easings by intent */
  --ease-out:  cubic-bezier(0.22, 1, 0.36, 1);  /* existing curve = enter */
  --ease-std:  cubic-bezier(0.2, 0, 0, 1);      /* things that stay on screen */
  --ease-exit: cubic-bezier(0.4, 0, 1, 1);      /* leaving: ~25% shorter */

  /* motion: semantic bundles (Atlassian-style) */
  --motion-press:      var(--dur-instant) var(--ease-std);
  --motion-sheet-in:   var(--dur-base) var(--ease-out);
  --motion-sheet-out:  calc(var(--dur-base) * 0.75) var(--ease-exit);
  --motion-step:       var(--dur-base) var(--ease-std);
  --motion-reveal:     var(--dur-slow) var(--ease-out);
}
```

The motion scale follows the systems that publish their values. **IBM Carbon uses 70/110 ms for micro-interactions, 150/240 ms for small expansions and toasts, 400 ms for large expansions and 700 ms for background dimming.** It separates "productive" motion for tasks from "expressive" motion reserved for rare significant moments ([Carbon motion](https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/motion/overview/)). Atlassian adds the rules that matter most here: anything triggered "dozens of times a day" stays under 150 ms, exits run faster than entrances, there is one focal motion at a time, and users never wait for an animation ([Atlassian motion](https://atlassian.design/foundations/motion)). Mapping the existing tokens: the current `--dur-fast` (0.18 s) sits between quick and base. `--dur` (0.35 s) is too slow for booking UI. `--dur-slow` (0.7 s) is ambient and belongs only on marketing pages. Keep the old names as aliases during migration so nothing breaks. The admin uses the same tokens with a `[data-surface="admin"]` scope that changes only density and type size (the owner wants bigger text) and limits itself to productive motion: instant and quick, never ambient. The gold rule carries over as one gold action per admin screen.

Two optional layers should wait. A seasonal accent driven by the fruit calendar is the safest form of data-driven theming, provided it tints only dividers, a badge or an illustration, and never text, actions or the gold button. Time-of-day palettes work on personal sites such as localghost.dev, but that author still reports a flash of the wrong colours in Firefox, and contrast in each stage is not handled ([localghost.dev](https://localghost.dev/blog/time-based-background-colour-transitions-with-temporal-and-css-color-mix/)). A monthly value set before the first paint avoids both problems. Dark mode is not worth the extra testing for a photo-led site used in daylight. The research found only agency opinion on its benefits, plus halation problems for readers with astigmatism ([Bop Design](https://www.bopdesign.com/bop-blog/2023/09/dark-mode-in-b2b-website-design/)). The semantic tier means it can be added later with one `light-dark()` block ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-us/docs/web/css/color_value/light-dark)).

## Native CSS covers nearly everything; add a library only for one job

Bundle size decides the library question on phones. Motion's full `motion` component is **about 43 KB and cannot be tree-shaken smaller**. `m` with `LazyMotion` starts at just over 5 KB, and `domAnimation` adds about 24 KB ([Motion docs](https://motion.dev/docs/react-reduce-bundle-size)). AutoAnimate is about 3.3 KB and animates list additions, removals and reordering with one hook ([skills.cat listing](https://skills.cat/skills/secondsky/claude-skills/auto-animate)). GSAP and all its former paid plugins are now free for commercial use, but under a **proprietary licence, not MIT** ([GSAP Standard License](https://gsap.com/community/standard-license/)). It earns its weight only for timeline storytelling, which this site does not need. The dotLottie player downloads a **~500 KB WASM engine before it renders anything** ([LottieFiles](https://docs.lottiefiles.com/en/runtimes/distributions/js/v0.x/core-concepts)), so it does not belong anywhere near the booking path. Lenis smooth scrolling is decoration that critics describe as sluggish for reading ([hontran.dev](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/nextjs-smooth-scroll-gsap-lenis)), and native momentum scrolling on phones is already good.

The platform now does most of the work. **`@starting-style` with `transition-behavior: allow-discrete` has been Baseline since August 2024.** That gives the PromptPay pop-up, the drop-down menu and the date sheet real entry and exit animations from `display: none` with no JavaScript ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/baseline-entry-animations)). **Same-document View Transitions became Baseline with Firefox 144 in October 2025** ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/same-document-view-transitions-are-now-baseline-newly-available)). But React Router's `viewTransition` prop works only in Data or Framework mode, not with the plain `<BrowserRouter>` this repo uses ([React Router](https://reactrouter.com/how-to/view-transitions)). React's own `<ViewTransition>` is still canary-only ([Chrome blog](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/view-transitions-in-2025)). For booking steps, which are state changes inside one route, the simplest route is to call `document.startViewTransition()` directly, with a check that it exists. Scroll-driven animations (`animation-timeline: view()`) reach about 88% of users, covering Chrome, Samsung and iOS 26+. Firefox support is very recent and disputed ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/mdn-css_properties_animation-timeline_view)). Use them only inside `@supports`, and only for the one or two marketing moments that earn them. Anchor positioning is still partial in most browsers ([caniuse](https://caniuse.com/css-anchor-positioning)) and should not be used for core menus. Whatever the tool, animate only `transform` and `opacity`. In web.dev's test, animating `top`/`left` dropped 50% of frames against 1% for transforms ([web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide)), and layout-property animations count toward layout shift ([web.dev CLS](https://web.dev/articles/debug-layout-shifts)). Accordions should use `grid-template-rows: 0fr → 1fr` rather than animating height.

The recommended stack is CSS and native APIs first, with **AutoAnimate (~3 KB) as the only optional dependency**, for lists such as the admin booking table, chat messages and announcements. Choose `m` + `LazyMotion` instead only if a future feature, such as a draggable orchard map, needs gestures. Never import the full `motion` component.

## The prioritised plan

The order is: remove what reads as AI and slows the task, then fix the tokens, then add the few motions that carry meaning. Show the owner mock-ups of items 7-9 before building, per the existing rule about big visual changes.

| # | Where | Change | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Booking, BookFlow, App, About | Remove the page-wide `fade-up` from booking steps entirely. On marketing pages keep at most one entrance per page (the hero text), cut to 400 ms and 8-12 px. | Removes the most-cited AI tell; 0.7 s delays reading on the task path |
| 2 | Every button and card | Shared `:active` press state (shade plus `scale: .97`, `--motion-press`). Disable on submit and change the label at once. Delay any spinner by about 400 ms. | First-frame feedback keeps INP low; stops double taps |
| 3 | `index.css` | Replace the blanket 0.01 ms reduced-motion rule. Write movement inside `no-preference`. Under `reduce`, keep colour and opacity changes and functional indicators. | Matches W3C 2.3.3 without killing feedback |
| 4 | `tokens.css` | Add the three tiers above. Replace the 43 hard-coded gold values with `--color-cta-*`. Map the old motion names to the new scale. Grep for raw `hsl(` in components. | One gold button stays one gold button; the theme stops drifting |
| 5 | Booking steps | Totals update in place with a ~200 ms number tween. Selected room gets a green border and check. Validate on blur with persistent fix-it text. Show all-in price from step 1. Guest checkout only. "Message us / LINE" visible at every step. | Baymard: price trust, inline validation, host contact |
| 6 | PromptPay pop-up | `@starting-style` entrance (240 ms) and faster exit. Large fixed amount and payee name, save-QR button, staged slip-check text with expected wait and fallback, persistent confirmed state with the booking code. | The trust moment; no toasts, no confetti |
| 7 | Activities | The fruit calendar is the site's living element: current month highlighted, "updated [date]" shown, a short state change when the guest taps a month. Optional `--color-season` accent on dividers only. | Real data changing, like Hollin and Trevaskis |
| 8 | Home hero | One real farm photo chosen by Bangkok time (morning / afternoon / dusk), crossfading only when it changes. No autoplay slideshow, no Ken Burns zoom, no video. | Honest "alive" signal; avoids the WCAG 2.2.2 burden |
| 9 | About (optional) | A family timeline drawn on scroll with `animation-timeline: view()` inside `@supports`, static elsewhere. Later, a tappable orchard map in the spirit of Chartogne-Taillet. | One expressive moment, like Tengile's timeline |
| 10 | Stay page and chat | Instant chat bubble as "sending…", then "sent ✓", with retry. Slideshow either does not autoplay or gets a visible pause. Arrows next to every swipe. | Optimistic UI only where it is safe; WCAG 2.2.2 |
| 11 | Admin | Same tokens, `[data-surface="admin"]` for bigger type and density. Instant and quick motion only. AutoAnimate for row changes. One gold action per screen. | Productive vs expressive split, as in Carbon |

There is also a list of things never to add: fade-up on every section, scroll-jacking or Lenis, parallax, custom cursors or magnetic buttons, marquees of words like "Fresh • Organic • Farm", letter-by-letter headline reveals, preloaders, shimmer skeletons, autoplay carousels without a pause, confetti, vibration, and purple or blue gradient glows. The booking flow follows six UX rules. Dates come first and above the fold, with booked nights clearly greyed out. The full price is shown from the first step, with no new fees later. No account or password. One gold action per screen. Feedback appears next to the finger within 100 ms and stays until it has been read. And there is always a human way out, LINE or chat, at every step.

## Conclusion

On this site, "lively" and "calm" are not a trade-off, because they belong to different places. The booking path feels alive when it answers instantly and stays put. The marketing pages feel alive when they show something that really changed: this month's fruit, this hour's light, this family's years on the land. Neither needs a new framework. The work that matters most is unglamorous: fewer fade-ups, a shared press state, a token file that makes the gold rule hard to break, and a reduced-motion rule that keeps feedback. That costs fewer kilobytes than any animation library.

The main uncertainty is evidential, not technical.
